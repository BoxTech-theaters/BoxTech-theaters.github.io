Insulation atlas/Air

One duct without a silencer defeats a theatre wall and the NR curve.

Quiet in the hall is a background-noise figure, not an insulation rating. And insulation between two rooms falls if they share a short air branch.

NR and NC

Inside the house, the quiet of the fan is an NR or NC curve, not an Rw. A speech theatre often sits between NR 20 and NR 25. A recording or dubbing room approaches NR 15. A classroom tolerates more, around NR 30 to 35 in much guidance, and the code’s figure replaces that. An open stadium is not governed by NR in the bowl; an enclosed suite is. High duct velocity adds self-noise even when a silencer is present.

Crosstalk

Two branches from one box between classrooms, or between a control room and the house, sometimes carry speech more clearly than a weak wall. The remedy is an independent branch, a silencer at each outlet, or a longer path with a lined bend. Pressure drop is calculated with the acoustics, not after. A supply opening directly above the door is a shortcut. A generator beside the house needs mass, a door, intake and exhaust silencers, and a resilient base. An absorbing ceiling in the house does not treat the generator.

  • Measure with the air system at the agreed speed, not switched off.
  • Visible wool in a penetration is absorption, not a plug. The plug is mass and a dense sealant.

A planning range from practice and the standards named, not a laboratory certificate in the name of BOXTECH ISO.
