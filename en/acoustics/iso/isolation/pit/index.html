Insulation atlas/Theatre · pit

The orchestra pit is open to the house on purpose. Insulation is to what sits under it and beside it.

The drum and the trombone are not isolated from the audience. They are isolated from a room under the pit, from the backstage corridor, and from a neighbouring hall on the same corridor.

What is drawn

The pit is part of the house, acoustically. A DnT,w between the pit and the seats is a figure without a meaning. The figure that is written is between the pit and a room under or beside it, if that room is used while the orchestra plays: a rehearsal room, a dressing room, a small studio, or a store that opens onto a quiet corridor. The slab under the players carries impact. If a room sits under the pit, the line is L'nT,w, not the side wall’s Rw alone. A corridor that runs from the pit to the foyer needs an acoustic doorset, or the flanking path is clearer than the house wall.

  • The piano lift and the load opening are a hole if left without a heavy lid and a seal.
  • The pit’s air duct is not shared with a quiet room.
  • Low frequency is asked for in the curve, not in a single number.

After the shell

Absorption on the pit wall sets the close reflection the player hears. It does not raise the insulation towards the room below. The absorber catalogue serves this second line. The first line stays a slab, a joint and a door.

A planning range, checked against the edition, the code and a measurement. Not a laboratory certificate in the name of BOXTECH ISO.
