Insulation atlas/Glass

Glass limits the suite, the control room and the façade. Thickness alone is not enough.

A single thick pane meets coincidence and then stops. Unequal double glass, a wide cavity and a closed frame, is the road for suites facing a pitch or a street.

Coincidence

Every glass pane has a frequency where insulation falls because the wave matches the bending of the pane. More thickness shifts that frequency; it does not delete it. Single 10 mm glass often sits around Rw 33–36. Two panes of unequal thickness — 6 with 10, or heavier — miss the same coincidence. A wide air cavity, towards 50–100 mm on sensitive façades, raises performance if the frame does not bridge the panes.

Where glass shows up

A theatre control room looks into the house. A stadium suite looks into the bowl. A classroom on a busy street looks onto traffic. In all three, glass is an isolating element named in the schedule: the build-up, the cavity, the frame, and whether the figure is the laboratory unit or the façade after installation. A curtain and an internal absorber treat the room’s echo. They do not raise the façade Rw.

  • Ordinary residential double glazing with a narrow cavity is weak against a bowl.
  • Vent slots in the façade frame cancel a large part of the unit’s figure.
  • An acoustic closer at the head of the wall if the façade runs past it, or the façade is a flanking path.

A planning range from practice and the standards named, not a laboratory certificate in the name of BOXTECH ISO.
