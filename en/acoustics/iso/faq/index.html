BOX TECH/Acoustics/BoxTech iso reference/Questions from professionals

Questions

What gets asked before the drawing is sent.

Are the products sold to private individuals?+–

No. The offer is aimed exclusively at entrepreneurs and professionals, as the original site states. The online shop, under its published terms, contracts with business customers in the European Union and a delivery address in Germany.

What is the difference between absorption and insulation?+–

Absorption shortens the tail of sound inside the same room. Insulation stops sound passing into another room. BOXTECH ISO is an absorption family. If speech must be stopped through a shared wall, that is a different construction line.

Is every product class A and fire class A1?+–

No. Absorption class A is stated on the board S24/R24-C datasheet. Building-material class A1 is stated for cover textile absorbers, in Lindner’s product description. Each system is attached to its own evidence at tender.

Does the calculator replace a laboratory test?+–

No. It is a Sabine planning estimate: volume, surfaces, and absorbing area. Lindner holds more than 150 tested measurements for comparison when a specification becomes formal. Use the calculator to choose a direction, then ask for data on the chosen system.

Can a colour or shape be ordered outside the catalogue?+–

Yes, within project manufacture: sizes, geometries, and cloths with or without frames. Digital print is available on a board sample, listed at €54. A free shape does not remove the need for enough absorbing area.

How are ceiling absorbers fixed?+–

Fixed direct to the soffit, or hung on wires when a shadow gap and room for services are wanted. Baffles are always suspended, because they work on two faces. Load details are checked with the engineer of the existing slab.

What is the recycling claim?+–

The English product text describes board as made from up to 95% recyclable polyester. The S24 sheet says the absorber is fully recyclable and reusable. That is not a claim that every textile family carries the same certificates.

Is there data in pCon.planner?+–

Yes. The BOXTECH ISO catalogue is available to furniture planners in pCon.planner, which shortens the gap between the object in the drawing and the line in the schedule.

Who makes them?+–

Manufacture is in Arnstorf, Lower Bavaria, within a family group established in 1965. During the trial the street address, telephone, email and the original website link are not published.

How do I enquire about a project outside Germany?+–

The form on the contact page prepares the project text on the device and does not open a mailbox during the trial. Include approximate volume, use (speech, dining, circulation), and any fire requirement. A sample is one thing; a project quotation is another.

Does a class A panel insulate a theatre or a classroom?+–

No. Class A is an absorption coefficient. Insulation of a theatre or a classroom is a DnT,w or Rw of a wall, a door and glass. The atlas is the insulation reference; the panel is in the systems catalogue.

Which figure is written between two classrooms?+–

Often DnT,w around 45 dB in school guidance, including the BB93 tradition, and higher for a music room. It is a planning range, replaced by the project code. The door and the duct are usually the limit.

Is an open stadium insulated with an Rw?+–

No. An open bowl has no wall to the sky. The limit is a level at the neighbour and the aim of the sound system. Rw belongs to the suite glass, the locker rooms and the generator.

Why does an operable wall’s figure collapse on site?+–

Because the laboratory measures the leaf with its track, and the site leaks at the suspended ceiling, the pocket and the floor. Ask for DnT,w after installation, and close the void above the track up to the slab.

Are the atlas figures BOXTECH ISO certificates?+–

No. They are planning ranges from standards and practice so a measurable line can be written. An absorber’s evidence stays on that system’s sheet, such as class A on board S24/R24-C.
